Smart Energy Management for a Hybrid Solar–Diesel–Battery Microgrid in Rural Yemen

An integrated microgrid design for rural electrification, combining photovoltaic generation, battery energy storage, diesel backup, rule-based energy management, industrial control, simulation, and supervisory monitoring.

Project Overview

The project addresses unreliable and costly electricity supply in rural coastal communities in Yemen. It proposes a standalone hybrid microgrid in which PV generation is prioritized, battery storage supports the system when required, and a diesel generator provides backup when renewable generation and allowable battery support are insufficient.

System Architecture

The proposed architecture combines approximately 150 kW of PV generation, battery energy storage, a 100 kW diesel generator, a common AC distribution bus, and three prioritized feeders. The EMS coordinates source selection and load allocation according to resource availability and feeder priority.

PV Generation

Primary renewable source used whenever sufficient solar power is available.

Battery Storage

Buffers renewable variability and supports critical loads during supply deficits.

Diesel Backup

Activated when PV and allowable battery support cannot preserve critical service.

Priority Feeders

F1 critical, F2 medium priority, and F3 low priority for controlled load shedding and restoration.

EMS & Control

Rule-based logic coordinates PV, battery, generator operation, SOC limits, and feeder states.

Supervisory Layer

Node-RED and a browser-based dashboard provide monitoring and bounded supervisory control.

Visual illustrations are provided to communicate the project structure and simulation concept; they are not photographs of a field-commissioned installation.

Simulation & Evaluation

MATLAB scripts and Simulink/Stateflow models were used to represent 24-hour PV and load profiles, EMS decisions, battery SOC behavior, generator commands, and feeder enable signals. The project evaluated representative renewable-dominant, moderate-deficit, and battery-supported operating scenarios.

Renewable-Dominant Operation

PV and battery resources supply the community with minimal or no diesel involvement under favorable conditions.

Priority-Based Curtailment

Lower-priority feeders are curtailed first while critical service is preserved as far as resources allow.

Generator Support

The diesel generator acts as a backup resource when PV and allowable battery discharge cannot maintain critical supply.

Control & Monitoring

The supervisory design connects EMS logic with a Node-RED middleware layer and a browser-based HTML dashboard. The dashboard presents system mode, battery SOC, PV and load values, generator status, feeder states, EMS parameters, and alarms, while bounded control actions are passed through the supervisory interface.

Project Scope

The work demonstrates the design, simulation, supervisory implementation, and functional validation of the proposed architecture. Full field commissioning, detailed protection coordination, site acceptance testing, cybersecurity hardening, and long-term field monitoring remain outside the scope of the study.
